Growing up in Cameroon, I have lived with the consequences of a line drawn in 1916 by two European diplomats who barely knew the land they divided.

That line became my first serious project. The Line is a digital history of Cameroon’s partition, reunification and colonial memory, which I research, write, design and code myself. Along the way I found I enjoy the building as much as the research: turning sources into maps that draw themselves, timelines you can search, and pages that respect a reader’s data plan.

I studied International Relations at the University of Yaoundé I, and I work as a freelance web developer, taking client sites from design to deployment. I bring the same approach to every project, including an exam-prep platform for students sitting the GCE, Baccalauréat and IELTS.

  • Never inventEvery fact sourced, every image licence-checked and credited, every uncertainty labelled.
  • Build for the real readerLow-end phones, slow data, accessibility, reduced-motion support.
  • Keep it simpleNo frameworks unless they earn their place. Content in plain files anyone can edit.
  • Respect peopleNo tracking, no cookies, consent first in oral history.

01The Line

Partition, Reunification & Colonial Memory in Cameroon. A nine-chapter digital history from German rule (1884) through the Anglo-French partition, UN trusteship, the 1961 plebiscite and the 1972 unitary state, plus how Cameroon, Africa and Europe remember it today.

  • Primary sources checked word for word: League of Nations and UN texts, the British mandate, Hansard (1961), INA archives
  • Scroll-driven maps, the 1949 Colonial Office map georeferenced over a modern map, an animated map of African independence
  • Oral-history strand with consent options; my own zero-dependency Node build; no cookies, no tracking
  • HTML
  • CSS
  • Vanilla JS
  • Node.js
  • SVG cartography
  • Archival research

02Examina Global

Exam preparation, in English and French. A platform for students in Cameroon preparing for the GCE O/A-Levels, the Baccalauréat and IELTS, organised by stream: Science or Arts, Academic or General Training.

Process

How I work
  1. 01

    Research

    Start from the sources and the real question: archives, readers, users. Every fact checked, every uncertainty flagged.

  2. 02

    Design

    Design for the real reader: a low-cost phone, slow data, two languages. Structure and type before decoration.

  3. 03

    Build

    Lean code that lasts: static where possible, frameworks only where they earn their place. Accessible and fast by default.

  4. 04

    Ship & verify

    Publish, test on real devices, check every link and source, then keep improving in the open.
