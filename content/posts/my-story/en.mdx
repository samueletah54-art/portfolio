I studied International Relations at the University of Yaoundé I. It was not a computer science degree, but it taught me two things I use every day: how to research a question properly, and how to explain it clearly to someone who was not in the room.

## Ghostwriting taught me to listen

In 2025 I started working as a ghostwriter. Clients would give me an idea and a deadline, and I would turn it into writing they could publish under their own name. Ghostwriting teaches you to listen first, to match someone else's voice, and to deliver on time.

## Learning to code by shipping

Around the same time I started teaching myself to code. I did not learn from a single course. I learned by building things people could use:

- **Examina Global**, an exam-prep platform for Cameroonian students in English and French
- **Mboa Phones**, a bilingual storefront for a phone shop in Yaoundé
- **The Line**, an interactive history of Cameroon's partition with maps and a timeline

## Building for Cameroon changes how you build

Most visitors are on low-cost Android phones and pay for every megabyte, so a fast, light page is not a nice extra: it decides whether someone sees your site at all. Many readers switch between English and French, so bilingual is the default. And because clients trust you with their business, security has to be built in from the start, which is why I am now deepening my skills in web security.

## Two jobs, one job

Today I build websites for businesses, schools and organisations, and I still write. The two jobs turn out to be the same job: understand what people need, then make it clear and easy for them. If you have a project, I would like to hear about it.
